The [search call site](https://github.com/official-pikafish/Pikafish/blob/9b963f727983a1d9308e0dca48b39c802b8e75a2/src/search.cpp#L585) skips this rule test at the root of normal search. A UCI `go` reply is therefore not an exact adjudication oracle. The future comparison must use an isolated diagnostic executable built in the external GPL source tree that invokes the helper directly and reports structured results. Keep that executable/source modification outside our repository, record its diff/hash/license, and never mistake a search evaluation for a referee verdict.

The [classical branch at 23b9466c981f0f3a1133f92de1a6f86406c4eccc](https://github.com/official-pikafish/Pikafish/blob/23b9466c981f0f3a1133f92de1a6f86406c4eccc/src/position.cpp#L1204) differs: `is_repeated` can return on the first earlier matching state, including when passed ply zero. ## State and history contract

- Record the public board, side to move, dark markers/starting roles, public check status after each completed reveal, and a no-capture counter at every real ply. No search null moves occur in a referee history.
- Give physical pieces stable identifiers that follow their moves. Chase intersections concern the same target piece, not merely a square or piece type. Swapping two same-type pieces may preserve a board position while changing which physical target was attacked.
- A repetition position key excludes the ply number, no-capture counter and full observation-history string. Our information-set cache key deliberately includes history and must not be reused as the repetition key.
- The pinned engine's position hash includes public piece placement/darkness, side to move and rest counts. During a capture-free, reveal-free cycle those counts cannot change. A diagnostic must feed an actor's actual visible candidate pools, not the referee's secret own pool. Prove equal verdicts for both observations of equivalent public cycles.
- Count only real moves. Capture resets the 80-ply counter; a noncapture reveal, soldier advance, check or evasion does not. A capture lowers total board population and a reveal lowers total dark population, so neither can belong to a cycle returning to the identical public position. These monotonic facts justify a separate repetition-history boundary without silently changing the no-capture clock.

## Repetition and responsibility

The pinned helper examines earlier states at even distances starting at four, bounded by the number of plies since a capture or search null move. At search-ply zero, it requires two earlier matching keys: the current position's third occurrence. Use the interval back to the second earlier match; do not classify responsibility using only the most recent short cycle if it differs from the earlier one. Search-only twofold shortcuts are excluded from referee rules.

For that interval, a side is a perpetual checker if every one of its relevant moves gives check. If exactly one side is a perpetual checker, that side loses. If both qualify, the repetition is drawn. If neither qualifies, classify continuous chase as below. Within that chase classification, an intervening check causes a draw in the newer pinned fallback; do not invent a generic “check or chase on every move” loss rule.

Repetition is examined before the 80-ply no-capture rule in the pinned helper. Thus an otherwise qualifying perpetual-check loss is not replaced with a draw when the third occurrence also reaches 80 plies. If repetition does not decide, the 80-ply rule draws when at least one legal move exists; no legal move loses. A legal repeated cycle already provides a legal outgoing move, so an apparent repetition/no-legal-move conflict indicates a malformed fixture or missing history. A complete referee should adjudicate a terminal state before accepting another move.

## Threats that count as chase in this fallback

This is a special threat classification, not an evaluation material score. It uses visible piece types and the positional role of a still-dark piece, not hidden true identities. Test its hypothetical captures independently from ordinary legal move generation: the pinned helper has its own king-safety operation.

1. Kings and soldiers are excluded as chasing attackers. Enemy kings are not chased targets. For an unpinned attacker, enemy soldiers that have not crossed the river are excluded; crossed enemy soldiers can qualify. A pinned attacker is instead restricted to nonking pieces identified as its pinners, followed by the hypothetical king-safety check.
2. A geometrical capture must preserve the attacker's king safety, including the facing-kings restriction. The newer helper's `checkers_to` includes enemy kings among rook-ray checkers, so flying general is covered despite lacking the older helper's separate guard.
3. Horse or cannon attacks on a chariot qualify even if the chariot has a legal recapture defender. Advisor or elephant attacks on a chariot, cannon or horse likewise qualify even if protected. These are discrete source rules, not inequalities derived from arbitrary piece values.
4. For other targets, simulate the candidate capture and look for any legal defending recapture onto that square. A defender whose recapture exposes its own king does not provide protection. If at least one qualifying recapture exists, this threat is excluded.
5. For an unprotected same-type target, a mutual legal capture normally excludes chase. A horse whose reverse attack is blocked by its leg, or a reverse capture that violates king safety, can still be chased. Different-type unprotected targets are not subject to the same-type exemption.
6. Classify all attackers of the moving side before and after each move. A target qualifies for that move only if it is a qualifying threat afterward and was not a qualifying threat beforehand. This admits discovered attacks and newly unprotected targets, while excluding merely maintaining an existing threat. A different attacker renewing pressure on a target already threatened by another attacker does not make that target new.
7. Intersect those newly threatened physical target IDs over all of a side's moves in the repetition interval. A nonempty intersection means continuous chase of at least one fixed target. Exactly one continuous chaser loses; both or neither means a draw.

## Source quirks requiring probes

These are source-derived risks, not runtime findings. Do not silently promote them to Tiantian facts or change the fallback to hide a disagreement.

| Probe | Why it matters | Current handling |
|---|---|---|
| Dark advisor threatening outside its own palace | Normal `legal` explicitly restricts a dark advisor's first move; `chased` uses advisor attacks and `chase_legal` lacks that explicit palace guard | Named `chase_hypothetical_dark_advisor_scope` option; pinned-helper behavior is the provisional value, pending a direct probe |
| Dark hypothetical captures and recaptures | Chase simulation uses the current positional type and does not resolve a chance reveal | Named deterministic positional-type convention; prove that no hidden identity enters a verdict |
| Pinned attacker and soldier exclusion | Pinned and unpinned paths use different target filters | Include a pinned-soldier-target counterexample rather than assuming the ordinary soldier exemption covers both paths |
| Mixed intermittent checking and chasing | New helper abandons chase classification on an intervening check; old helper has different safety semantics | Separate versioned profiles; test both, do not combine |
| Stable target identity across same-type exchanges | Board-key equality does not identify a particular physical target | Test alternating targets and cycles that exchange identical pieces |
| Third occurrence at the no-capture threshold | Repetition responsibility precedes the clock draw | Concrete perpetual-check fixture starts at 72 plies and repeats twice |
